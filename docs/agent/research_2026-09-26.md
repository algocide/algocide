# Research layer: regime and candidate setups (2026-09-26)

**Purpose.** The BRAIN's slow-layer output for the reflex agent in `src/hlagent/`: a regime call, a graded list of
candidate setups, and the typed specs the reflex loop consumes (`config/setups_2026-09-26.json`).

**How to read the evidence labels.** *measured* = tested in this repository on real observations (see
`docs/decision_memo.md`, `docs/review_adversarial.md`); *reported* = a dated primary or secondary source, cited inline;
*speculation* = a mechanism story with no measurement. Every number below is either a citation or labelled as an
estimate. Nothing here is a forecast of profit; the repository's own conclusion of 2026-09-24 ("no credible,
economically meaningful edge was found") stands until forward data says otherwise.

**What could not be checked from this session.** The network policy blocks `api.hyperliquid.xyz`, every exchange and
data API, the TypeSafe (Jev) and AgenKit sites, and the third-party write-ups of Jev. Live Hyperliquid funding, open
interest and order-book depth were therefore not observed; the venue numbers below come from dated news and search
snippets and must be re-read live before any capital moves.

### 2.1 Notes on the two setups the loop may paper-trade

**hype_carry.** Supply: HYPE unlocks will roughly triple circulating supply over time and the fee burn (4.9% of
supply so far) only partly offsets it (AOL, 2026-09-26). Revenue: about $820M yearly as of April 2026, with 99% of
protocol revenue routed to HYPE buybacks (Hyperliquid Strategies conference report, 2026-09-15; AOL 2026-09-26).
Holder concentration: treasury companies (PURR, Lion Group) are large, price-insensitive buyers whose demand depends
on their own capital raising (AOL, 2026-09-26). Value accrual to the token is unusually direct (buyback-and-burn),
which is why the carry has speculative-long demand to feed on, and why funding compresses when that demand cools.
*Invalidation in code:* funding negative for 72 consecutive hourly ticks, or a 5% book drawdown. *What kills it:* a
bear regime, a fee change, or having to run the perp leg at 2x.

**xyz_premium_reversion_nvda.** The adversarial review showed the reversion is the AR(1) half-life of an hourly
average premium, not a tradeable dislocation at standard fees. With growth-mode fees confirmed (reported, not
verified against `userFees`), the pre-declared forward test in `docs/forward_plan.md` (60 events, mean net >= +8
bps with a bootstrap lower bound above zero, median half-spread <= 3 bps) can start on liquid US equities in the
external session. A 3x deployer fee increase, if enacted on those names, ends it. *Invalidation in code:* observed
spread above 10 bps for three ticks.

## 3. Compiled decision schema

The reflex layer answers five typed questions per candle (`src/hlagent/schema.py::JEV_QUESTIONS`):

```
regime         choice   trending | mean_reverting | high_vol | crisis
direction      choice   long | short | neutral          (with a probability for the chosen direction)
toxic_flow     yes_no
setup_quality  score    0..3
risk_state     choice   safe | near_limit | reduce
```

and the code gates on `setup_quality >= 2`, `confidence > 0.80`, `risk_state == safe` from BOTH the judge and the
risk layer, `toxic_flow == False`, spread and staleness limits, and setup validity; it sizes with fractional Kelly
capped at quarter Kelly and at 10% of equity per market (`src/hlagent/policy.py`). Each finalist is a `SetupSpec`
(`src/hlagent/setups.py`) with a fixed coin, bias, horizon and invalidation rules the code evaluates every tick.
